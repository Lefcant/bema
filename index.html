        Prompts

          File type

          ContainerWAV, uncompressed PCM. soundfile, torchaudio and pydub all read it directly, with no ffmpeg step.
          Sample rate48 kHz, the rate of your Aristotle clips. IMS-Toucan resamples to 16 kHz itself and the VITS notebook to 22.05 kHz, so the master stays at 48.
          Depth, channels16-bit, mono.
          ProcessingNone. The browser's echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain are switched off, and nothing is normalised or denoised on export.

          Length

          • Aim for prompts of 3 to 9 seconds. The aligner's ceiling is MAX_CLIP_SEC = 10, and IMS-Toucan skips files shorter than 1 s or longer than 15 s.
          • The Script tab cuts at sentence ends first, then at commas, colons and dashes, as the aligner's packer does, and at brackets.
          • Keep one passage under about 10 minutes of audio. The aligner's memory need grows with the square of the file length: roughly 0.3 GB for 10 minutes, 3 GB for 30.

          One project folder per voice. File names follow the aligner: <parent_id>_<NNN>. Text is written exactly as you pasted it; only runs of white space are collapsed.

          At the microphone

          • Same microphone, same distance, same room every session. Consistency matters more than polish for a single voice.
          • Set the gain so that your loudest syllables peak between -12 and -6 dBFS.
          • After pressing record, wait for the red light, then read. Leave a beat of silence before stopping. The first 0.3 s and the last 0.12 s are dropped to remove the key click.
          • Bluetooth headsets send narrow-band audio. Use a wired or USB microphone.
          • Stop when your voice tires. The timbre shifts and the takes stop matching the rest.

          On a phone

          • A phone gives the microphone only to a page opened from an https:// address, never to a downloaded file, whatever its permission settings say. So put this one file on a web host. With GitHub Pages: make a public repository, upload the file as index.html, switch Pages on under Settings (branch main), then open https://<name>.github.io/<repository>/ on the phone and allow the microphone when asked.
          • Prop the phone up 20 to 30 cm from your mouth and do not hold it: every touch travels through its body into the microphone. Recording stops the moment your finger lands on the button, so the tap itself stays out of the take.
          • A phone has no input gain to set. Move it closer or further until your loudest syllables peak between -12 and -6 dBFS, then keep that distance.
          • Which of its microphones a phone uses differs by model. Read a line with each end towards you and keep the position with the better level and the lower room noise.
          • Switch on Do Not Disturb, so that nothing rings or buzzes during a take, and disconnect Bluetooth earphones: with them connected, a phone may record through their microphone in telephone quality.
          • The screen stays awake while the microphone is on. Left alone for ten minutes, the microphone switches itself off.
          • A phone shapes the sound in ways a web page cannot switch off. Listen to a few takes before you record many.
          • Takes are kept in the browser's storage for this address. Export each passage when it is done and move the zip to your computer or drive.

          Where recordings are kept

          In Chrome or Edge you can pick a project folder. Every kept take is then written there at once as a WAV file, and exports land in the same folder. If the folder sits in a synced drive, Colab can read it from there without uploads.

          Without a folder, takes stay in this browser's storage and each passage exports as a zip. Clearing site data removes them, so export as you go.

          Nothing is sent anywhere. The page makes no network requests.

          What a take is checked for

          • Clipping, and peaks within 1 dB of full scale.
          • Speech touching the start or end of the take, which means a cut-off sound.
          • A click set apart from the speech: a key, the mouse, the lips.
          • Room noise too close to your voice for the aligner's trim gate (35 dB below the loudest frame, never below -55 dBFS).
          • Length outside 1 to 10 s, pauses over a second, a level more than 6 dB from your usual one, and a pace far from your own average, which usually means a skipped or repeated word.

          It cannot hear a misread word. That check is yours: play the take when in doubt.
